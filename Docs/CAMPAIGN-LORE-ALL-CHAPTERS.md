Extracted verbatim from Pinoy's CAMPAIGN drive, one `CHAPTER LORE.rtfd` bundle per chapter,
converted from RTF to plain text. Content is unedited; only RTF control codes were stripped.
Each chapter folder also holds an OG folder with 14 enemy PNGs (84 total, about 1300x1200 RGBA, 139.9 MB).

1. The Forgotten Road - boss The Collector
2. The Abandoned Crossing - miniboss The Waykeeper, boss The Shepherd

# ================ CHAPTER 1 ================

I think Chapter 1 should be
very grounded
. The player has just watched an apocalyptic battle, the mysterious rewind happens, then instead of immediately explaining anything:

silence. birds. morning.

The contrast sells the rewind.

Chapter 1 — The Forgotten Road

Where do we "wake up"?

I'd have the first hero wake up beside an
old roadside shrine on the edge of a forest
, overlooking a distant village.
Important distinction:
the player doesn't physically wake up.

The first hero does.
The player's awareness returns
inside/alongside that hero's thoughts
.
The hero doesn't remember travelling there. They know their name and who they are, but there's a strange hole in their recent memory.
Then they hear something:

Wake up.

The hero opens their eyes.
They assume it was their own thought.
But that's actually
the player
.
That's the first canonical interaction between the Voice and a hero.
The player recognizes nothing after the chaos of the tutorial.

Chapter 1 — 12 Stages

I'd make Chapter 1 one relatively contained journey:

Shrine  Road  Village  surrounding countryside  abandoned settlement  old ruins  first major threat  leave for the wider world.

Stage 1 — The Roadside Shrine

Purpose: Beginning

The hero wakes beside the shrine.
Their belongings are nearby. They remember their identity, combat training, etc., but
not why they're here
.
They see smoke rising from a village in the distance.
Not disaster smoke — normal chimneys.
With nowhere else to go, they head toward it.

Stage ending:
 The hero reaches the road and encounters someone being attacked.
This lets Chapter 1 naturally transition from tutorial  normal campaign gameplay.

Stage 2 — The Stranger on the Road

Purpose: First connection

The hero saves a traveller/merchant from creatures or bandits.
Something weird happens during combat.
The hero instinctively moves exactly where the player tells them.
Afterward:

"I don't normally fight like that."
They dismiss it.
The traveller tells them about the nearby village.
Something has been wrong there lately.

Stage 3 — Alderwick

Purpose: Establish normal life

We reach our first proper settlement.

At first it looks completely normal.
Children playing. Market open. Blacksmith working. Inn busy.
But conversations feel... off.
Someone forgot an appointment.
Someone can't remember why they were arguing.
Someone recognizes their spouse but can't remember how they met.
Nothing catastrophic.
Yet.

Stage 4 — The Cold Forge

Purpose: Introduce belief loss

We meet the blacksmith.
He's stopped working.
Not because he forgot
how
.
He remembers every technique perfectly.
He simply can't remember:

Why he ever cared about making things.

This establishes an important distinction.
People aren't simply suffering from amnesia.
Something deeper has been damaged.

Stage 5 — Beyond the Gate

Purpose: Give the party an objective

Several villagers have recently disappeared.
Normally the village guard would search for them.
But the guards have strangely lost the motivation to continue.
Our hero volunteers.
Tracks lead into the surrounding forest.
Now Chapter 1 becomes a proper adventure.

Stage 6 — Those Who Left

Purpose: Escalation

The hero finds some missing villagers.
They're alive.
They're not prisoners.

They left voluntarily.

One remembers their family perfectly.

"I know they're waiting for me."
Hero:

"Then come home."

"Why?"
That should be the first genuinely unsettling moment.

Stage 7 — Another Voice

Purpose: Expand the party

We encounter another important hero.
They're investigating the same phenomenon for their own reasons.
During an attack, both heroes react simultaneously to one of the player's commands.
Afterward:

"You felt that too?"

"Felt what?"
Neither understands it.
This is the first hint that
the Voice isn't inside one specific character.

Stage 8 — The Empty Hamlet

Purpose: Show the scale

The trail leads to another small settlement.
Completely abandoned.
Meals were left on tables.
Doors weren't broken.
No battle.
Everyone simply
walked away
.
Now Alderwick clearly isn't an isolated incident.

Stage 9 — Beneath the Hill

Purpose: First ancient mystery

The heroes discover something beneath/near the abandoned settlement.
Old ruins.
Much older than anything around them.
Inside are carvings.
Among them:

six symbols.

We don't name Ember, Verdant, Tide, Gale, Umbra or Lumen.
Not yet.
One hero touches the wall and experiences a tiny flash of something impossible.
Perhaps a location from the tutorial.
Less than a second.
Then it's gone.

Stage 10 — The Hollowed

Purpose: First special enemies

Something has happened to some of the missing people.
They've lost so much conviction that they're almost empty versions of themselves.
Not zombies.
Not possessed.
They can speak.
They remember their names.
But there's almost
nothing driving them anymore
.
Someone/something has learned how to exploit this condition.
This gives us our first campaign-specific enemy type.

Stage 11 — The One Who Takes

Purpose: First antagonist subordinate

We discover someone has deliberately been gathering/exploiting these lost fragments of conviction.
Not the main antagonist.
A minor agent.
More importantly, they recognize something strange about our heroes.
They look at the first hero and say:

"Interesting."

"You're empty too."
Hero:

"What?"
Then they correct themselves:

"No... something else is occupying the space."

They're sensing
the player
.
That should be our first big Voice mystery.

Stage 12 — The First Echo

Chapter boss.

The heroes defeat this agent and stop whatever was happening around Alderwick.
Some affected people begin recovering.
But before the enemy disappears/dies, they see the six symbols.
And finally give us a tiny clue:

"You don't even remember them."
Hero:

"Remember what?"
They smile.

"What you believed."

Then they're gone.
The heroes return to Alderwick.
Things are improving — but reports have arrived.

Other settlements are experiencing the same thing.

Much farther away.
Our heroes decide to leave Alderwick and investigate.
And then I'd end Chapter 1 with something involving the player.

Final Chapter 1 scene

Night.
The two heroes sit beside a campfire outside Alderwick.
One finally brings up what happened earlier.

"Back there..."

"You moved before I said anything."
The other:

"So did you."
Silence.

"Did you hear it?"
The first hero looks uncomfortable.

"Hear what?"
And then...

the player hears something.

Not through either hero.
The same distorted presence from the rewind:

"...remember..."

Black.

Stage 6 Miniboss — The Waykeeper

I'd make this one quite tragic.

Race:
 Human
Role:
 Warden
Enemy Type:
 Miniboss
The Waykeeper once maintained
The Crossing
, an important checkpoint connecting Alderwick's region to the wider world.
Travelers paid tolls, guards protected merchants, and the Waykeeper was responsible for keeping the route safe.
Now the Crossing is nearly abandoned.
But he remains.
He remembers every rule.
Every toll.
Every procedure.
Every law.
What he's forgotten is
why those rules existed.

So they've become absolute.

"No passage without authorization."
Hero:
"Authorization from whom?"
Long silence.
"...No passage."
He attacks.
This is our first strong example of a belief becoming
detached from its purpose
 rather than simply disappearing.

Stage 12 Boss — The Shepherd

This is where I'd start escalating the mystery beyond The Collector.

Race:
 Human
Role:
 Binder
Enemy Type:
 Boss
The Shepherd isn't creating Hollowed people.
Instead, they've discovered something disturbing:

People who've lost their convictions are extremely easy to influence.

So rather than collecting what remains like
The Collector
, the Shepherd
gives them new purpose
.
That's why missing people have been walking toward The Crossing.
They're being gathered.
The Shepherd tells them:

"You were lost."
"I gave you direction."
And the affected people genuinely believe they're being helped.

Why this boss matters

The Collector taught us:

Conviction can disappear.

The Shepherd teaches us:

The empty space can be filled by someone else.

Which is MUCH more dangerous.
And it introduces a very important theme for the eventual player/Voice reveal.
Because what does the player do?

Influence heroes.

The Shepherd could notice this.
During Stage 12:

Shepherd:
"You condemn me for guiding them."
Looks toward your heroes.
"Yet none of you have noticed the irony."
The heroes don't understand.

"Something guides you too."

That should make the player slightly uncomfortable.
Because for the first time, the game is directly asking:

What's the difference between the Shepherd controlling people... and you controlling heroes?

We don't answer that until much later.
The eventual answer is
choice and trust
 — your heroes willingly form their connection with the Voice, whereas the Shepherd exploits people incapable of choosing.

Stage 12 Boss — The Arbiter

Here's where I'd introduce someone considerably more intelligent and dangerous than the Collector or Shepherd.

Race:
 Human
Role:
 Binder
Enemy Type:
 Boss
The Arbiter is an important city authority — judge, adviser, administrator or similar.
Unlike most affected people:

The Arbiter hasn't lost their conviction.

They've watched everyone else lose theirs.
And they've reached a conclusion:

People cannot be trusted to decide for themselves anymore.

Crime is increasing.
Guards abandon posts.
Parents abandon families.
Merchants break agreements.
Officials stop caring.
So the Arbiter begins replacing
personal conviction with rules
.
Absolute rules.
No interpretation.
No choice.
No disagreement.
And surprisingly...

the city starts functioning again.

That's what makes them interesting.

The Arbiter's city

Under their control:
Crime decreases.
Guards return to work.
Food distribution resumes.
Streets become safer.
But...
Nobody gets to choose anything.
People receive assigned work.
Movement is restricted.
Questioning orders becomes a crime.
Those whose convictions are particularly damaged are actually
relieved
.
Someone is finally telling them what to do.
The heroes initially aren't even sure the Arbiter is the enemy.

Connection to the Voice

Stage 6 Miniboss — The Standard Bearer

Human · Warden

A famous soldier carrying the banner of one of the territories.
The Standard Bearer has become a symbol to the troops. Soldiers who would otherwise desert continue fighting simply because
they see the banner still standing
.
The heroes try to explain that continuing the battle will only kill more people.
The response:

"If we lower the banner now, everyone who died beneath it died for nothing."

That's the trap.
Their conviction hasn't disappeared.
It's become impossible to abandon because abandoning it would mean admitting the sacrifices made for it may have been pointless.
Defeating the Standard Bearer doesn't magically end the war—but soldiers seeing the banner fall causes the first major collapse in morale.

Stage 12 Boss — The Peacemaker

Human · Binder

I'd deliberately give Chapter 5's boss a name that initially sounds
good
.
The Peacemaker is a powerful mediator who originally tried to end the conflict.
They negotiated.
They listened.
They compromised.
And every peace agreement failed.
Eventually they reached a conclusion:

There can be no conflict if everyone believes the same thing.

Rather than destroying conviction like we've seen before, the Peacemaker tries to
eliminate disagreement
.
They've begun gathering soldiers and civilians from both territories and forcing them toward a single shared doctrine.
Not Ironshield.
Not Freeblade.
Something supposedly "neutral."

Why they're dangerous

The Peacemaker can demonstrate something important about the Six.
The heroes have recently learned these ancient beliefs exist.
The Peacemaker has discovered fragments of the same knowledge.
But they've completely misunderstood its lesson.
To them:

Six beliefs created six ways to disagree.
Therefore:

Six must become one.

The heroes finally begin understanding the opposite.
Different beliefs existing isn't necessarily the problem.

Refusing to coexist with different beliefs is.

Connection to the Voice

This also pushes the player mystery forward.
The Peacemaker notices the heroes have different convictions yet somehow cooperate perfectly.

Peacemaker:
"Different beliefs."
"Different desires."
"Yet one will moves through all of you."
They become fascinated.

"You are what I have been trying to create."

That's disturbing because, from the outside, the player's party actually
does look like the Peacemaker's ideal society
.
Several individuals acting as one.
But we're slowly building toward the distinction:

The Peacemaker wants unity by removing choice.

The Voice creates unity despite choice.

We still don't fully explain that yet.

Stage 6 Miniboss — The Penitent

Undead Human · Warden

The Penitent was once a scholar of the Six.
They spent their life trying to determine:

Which belief was correct?

Ember?
Tide?
Verdant?
Gale?
Umbra?
Lumen?
Eventually they became obsessed with finding an objective answer.
They failed.
Rather than accepting that different convictions could coexist, they concluded that their own inability to choose meant they were
unworthy of believing anything
.
They remained at the sanctuary after everyone else disappeared.
Even after death.
When encountering the heroes, they ask each one why they believe what they believe.
Every answer is rejected.

"You cannot all be right."

One hero responds:

"Maybe we don't have to be."

That becomes an important thematic step after Chapter 5.
Defeating the Penitent gives the party access to the true sanctuary.

Stage 12 Boss — The Curator

Ancient Construct · Binder

The Curator was created to protect the teachings of all Six.
Its original command:

Preserve the Six.

But centuries without guidance caused the Curator to interpret that command literally.
It no longer allows beliefs to
change
.
Records cannot be altered.
Teachings cannot be questioned.
Interpretations cannot evolve.
Anyone entering the sanctuary and expressing an interpretation that differs from its archives is classified as a threat.
The Curator has essentially turned
belief into preserved information
.

The Six are finally named

Throughout Stages 7–11, the party finds fragments of teachings.
At Stage 12, the Curator finally provides the complete classification:

EMBER — Escalation

TIDE — Redirection

VERDANT — Persistence

GALE — Disruption

UMBRA — Removal

LUMEN — Definition

Not elements.
Not gods.
Not nations.
Not magical schools.

Ways people respond to the world.

This is the first time the player gets the proper framework.

The Curator and the Voice

The Curator analyzes the heroes.
It recognizes different beliefs among them.
But again, it cannot understand their connection.

Curator:
"Contradictory convictions detected."

"Cooperation should be unstable."
Yet the party remains together.

"Identify governing doctrine."

Nobody answers.
The Curator scans again.

"No governing doctrine detected."

That's important.
The Peacemaker believed the Voice was making everyone believe the same thing.
But the Curator discovers:

It isn't.

The heroes remain fundamentally different.
Something connects them without making them identical.
We still don't explain what that means.
